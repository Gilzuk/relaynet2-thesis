\section{Experiments Summary}
\label{sec:experiments-summary}

\paragraph{Role in the thesis.}
This chapter establishes the matched-model control against which the
unknown- and mismatched-channel results of
Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels} are interpreted.  The canonical
configuration is the half-duplex source--relay--destination link defined in
Chapter~\ref{sec:methods}: two SISO hops, one relay, no direct
source--destination path, independent Rayleigh fast fading on the two hops,
complex baseband, Gray-coded QPSK, white noise, coherent one-tap compensation,
and uncoded BER.  Relay processing is the varied block in the canonical
comparison.  The channel is memoryless and its required parameters are
available to the receiver chain, so neither identifiability nor sequence
detection is under pressure.

Experiments E2 and E3 constitute the canonical control.  E2 compares the
classical and learned relay functions.  E3 studies model-capacity saturation
and an approximately equal parameter budget.  E8 is a cross-scenario
capacity-and-context study supporting H3 and the role of channel memory; it
is not an additional layer of the regime map.  E7 is a supplementary
coded/resource study.  It measures the distinction in
Remark~\ref{rem:df-terminology} between uncoded symbol-wise DF and practical
finite-length block-DF, then examines soft information, throughput, MCS
selection, computation, and buffering delay.  E7 is likewise not an
information layer.  The principal H5 investigation begins only in
Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels}, where Layers~2--4 progressively reduce
the information available for model-based processing.

\section{Canonical Relay Comparison: E2}
\label{sec:siso-bpsk-performance-baseline-relay-comparison}

\paragraph{Setup and hypotheses.}
The comparison uses the canonical configuration without changing topology,
modulation, channel law, power convention, receiver information, or BER
metric.  The evaluated learned relays are the MLP, Hybrid, VAE, Transformer,
Mamba-S6, and Mamba-2 SSD, with ReLU hidden activations and tanh output.
H1 is tested as a bounded claim that learned nonlinear processing can improve
on AF, particularly at low SNR.  H2 asks whether any evaluated learned relay
reliably outperforms matched symbol-wise DF.  A numerical tie or a difference
within uncertainty does not reject H2.

\paragraph{Analytical validation.}
The abscissa is $E_s/N_0$.  For QPSK, $E_b/N_0$ is $3.01$~dB lower.
After this conversion, the two-hop expression $2P(1-P)$, with
\[
P=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\bar{\gamma}}{1+\bar{\gamma}}}\right),
\]
agrees with measured DF within a few percent at all tabulated points; the
largest printed relative discrepancy is approximately $3.6\%$ at 12~dB.
Figure~\ref{fig:fig10} also includes the single-hop curve $P$ as a
genie-aided reference: it is the hop-2 BER obtained when the transmitted
symbol is supplied perfectly to the relay under the same hop-2 model.  It is
not asserted as a universal lower bound for arbitrary relay protocols.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The feedforward learned relays closely track symbol-wise DF from 4~dB
upward and improve clearly on AF.  Thus H1 is supported relative to AF.
No evaluated learned relay establishes a reliable advantage over matched
symbol-wise DF; occasional lower printed means are interpreted as ties at
the available resolution.  This supports the bounded H2 statement.  The
sequence models trail at low SNR and converge toward the feedforward group
at high SNR, but E2 alone cannot separate parameter count, architecture,
context, and optimization effects.

\paragraph{Why QPSK.}
QPSK exercises both dimensions of the complex baseband model and is processed
by the I/Q-split relays defined in Chapter~\ref{sec:methods}.  Coherent BPSK
would also be valid after phase removal; the choice is experimental rather
than a correctness requirement.  BER values on an $E_s/N_0$ QPSK axis must
not be compared directly with BPSK values on an $E_b/N_0$ axis.

\section{Minimum Relay Size Across Channel Families: E8}
\label{sec:minimum-relay-size}

E8 sweeps window $\in\{1,3,5,7\}$, depth $\in\{1,2,3\}$, and width from
1 to 48 across the evaluated channel families and coded scenario.  Each
configuration is run with three initializations and judged by its worst
initialization.  The metric is additional SNR required to attain a specified
BER relative to the scenario's published relay.

Within the evaluated families, required context tracks channel memory more
directly than raw parameter count.  At zero degradation, three of five
memoryless scenarios are matched by four parameters, $42\times$ fewer than
MLP-169.  The three-tap scenarios require 73--145 parameters, and retain
windows of 5 or 7 until a full decibel of degradation is allowed.  Increasing
the window is worth 8--11~dB on those three-tap cases and is negligible on
the memoryless cases.  Increasing depth from one to three hidden layers
does not improve any reported minimum.  These are empirical findings over
the tested families, not a universal law connecting impulse-response length
and neural-network size.

The sweep finds no upward arm beyond initialization spread.  It therefore
strengthens the capacity-saturation interpretation of H3 while leaving the
stronger claim of overfitting unsupported.  The complete budget table and
window figures remain in Table~\ref{tbl:table-minsize} and
Figures~\ref{fig:minsize-crossover}--\ref{fig:minsize-budget}.

Soft read-out improves the MLP at every tabulated SNR.  The nominal BCJR

produces symbol-dependent effective noise variance.  Empirically, increasing
the assumed variance by a factor of two changes the 20~dB result from

The mechanistic interpretation is that posterior saturation makes the soft
output approach a hard constellation decision, while code-aware processing
at the relay has already consumed the redundancy later needed for repair.

about feasible choices within the measured grid, not global
latency-constrained capacity.

\section{Chapter Synthesis}

The canonical matched-model control supports H1 as a bounded advantage over
AF and supports H2 in its revised form: no evaluated learned relay reliably
outperforms matched symbol-wise DF.  E3 and E8 support H3 as capacity
saturation rather than demonstrated overfitting.  H4 is only partly
supported: equal parameter budgets reduce architecture-dependent differences
at high SNR, but do not remove the low-SNR separation, and structural model
quantities remain confounded.

The supplementary coded study changes the system-level ranking through code
rate, soft information, downstream repairability, MCS selection, runtime,
and buffering delay.  These results complement the regime map but do not add
an information layer.  Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels} next addresses H5
by introducing channel memory and progressively reducing the information
available for instantiating model-based classical rules, thereby testing the
identifiability boundary separately from the arithmetic boundary.
