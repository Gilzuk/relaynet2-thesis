\chapter{Discussion and Conclusions}
\label{sec:discussion-and-conclusions}

\paragraph{Scope of the discussion.}
The evidence discussed in this chapter comes from three distinct experimental blocks.
First, the canonical QPSK/Rayleigh study in Chapter~\ref{sec:experiments} is a
matched-model, memoryless, uncoded control.  It primarily settles H1--H4 by
comparing relay functions while the source, channels, coherent receiver
processing, and destination remain fixed.  Second, the supplementary
coded/resource study in the same chapter introduces convolutional coding,
block decode-and-forward (block-DF), soft-information forwarding, adaptive
modulation and coding, and explicit latency constraints.  It is a system-level
extension, not an additional layer of channel information.  Third,
Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels} contains Layers~2--4 of the
unknown/mismatched-channel study and is the principal experimental contribution
for H5.

The main contribution is therefore not a blanket learned-over-classical
victory.  It is a regime map with two boundaries.  The first is
\emph{identifiability}: whether a sufficiently accurate channel model or
per-block estimate is available.  The second is \emph{arithmetic and resource
feasibility}: whether exact model-based processing can be afforded once channel
memory and modulation order enlarge its state space.  Classical processing
dominates when its model is identifiable and its computation is affordable.
Learned windowed processing becomes useful when those preconditions cease to
hold, while remaining bounded by the strongest applicable classical benchmark.

\section{Interpretation of Results}
\label{sec:interpretation-of-results}

\subsection{Low-SNR performance and H1}

H1 is confirmed in its stated, limited form: on the canonical channel at
0--4~dB, the smallest effective learned relays improve materially over AF.
At 0~dB, Hybrid, MLP, and VAE obtain BERs of \(0.3329\), \(0.3349\), and
\(0.3359\), respectively, compared with \(0.4076\) for AF.  These values are
close to symbol-wise DF at \(0.3337\); they do not establish a general learned
advantage over DF.  The result is thus an advantage over linear noise
forwarding, not over the matched hard decision.

A posterior-mean interpretation provides qualitative motivation.  After the
canonical receiver uses CSI for coherent phase compensation, one real decision
axis can be written as
\[
    z = a x + n,\qquad
    x\in\{-1,+1\},\quad a=|h|,\quad n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2).
\]
For equiprobable symbols,
\begin{equation}
    f^\star(z,a)
    = \mathbb{E}[x\mid z,a]
    = \tanh\!\left(\frac{a z}{\sigma^2}\right).
    \label{eq:posterior-mean-discussion}
\end{equation}
The familiar \(\tanh(z/\sigma^2)\) form is the unit-gain AWGN special case.
This smooth confidence-valued mapping explains why a small nonlinear relay can
occupy a useful position between linear AF and hard slicing.  It is an
interpretive model, however, rather than proof that the trained network
implements the exact conditional mean.  CSI is already used by coherent
preprocessing; whether \(a=|h|\) is additionally presented as a separate
neural input is a different question.

The previously quoted AF expression
\(\gamma^2/(2\gamma+1)\) is not used here to explain the canonical Rayleigh
result.  That expression belongs to a particular equal-hop AF gain and SNR
convention, whereas the implemented canonical AF relay is power-normalized
over fading observations.  The measured AF comparison is sufficient and avoids
transferring an unverified \(3\)-dB statement between non-identical models.

\subsection{Matched memoryless processing and H2}

H2 is confirmed for the matched, uncoded, memoryless canonical problem.
From approximately 6~dB upward, symbol-wise DF matches or exceeds the learned
relays while requiring no learned parameters or training.  The later
minimum-size sweep places the residual learned penalty against DF at only
about \(0.05\)--\(0.09\)~dB.  Thus H2 is strong in direction but small in
measured margin.

The appropriate explanation is structural.  Once coherent compensation has
reduced the matched memoryless problem to independent per-axis decisions,
componentwise slicing is the matched symbol decision implemented by
symbol-wise DF.  The evidence does not identify finite numerical precision,
incomplete \(\tanh\) saturation, or larger-model variance as the cause of the
remaining small gap, so those explanations are not retained.

For the real-AWGN calibration convention used in the thesis,
\(\sigma^2=P_s/\gamma\), the single-hop BPSK error probability is
\(p=Q(\sqrt{\gamma})\), and two independent DF hops give
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{DF},2\mathrm{hop}}
      = 2p(1-p).
    \label{eq:two-hop-df-discussion}
\end{equation}
At \(\gamma=10\), this is
\(2Q(\sqrt{10})[1-Q(\sqrt{10})]\approx1.56\times10^{-3}\).
This value is correct only under that real-sample SNR convention.  Under the
more common convention in which the horizontal axis is \(E_b/N_0\), the
single-hop argument is \(\sqrt{2E_b/N_0}\); the two numerical statements must
not be interchanged.

\subsection{Robustness across the fading ensemble}

The canonical results show stable performance across i.i.d. Rayleigh draws
within the evaluated distribution.  They should not be described as
model-agnostic generalization or online adaptation.  Coherent receiver
processing uses the channel coefficient before neural inference, while
multi-SNR training exposes the fixed network to the range of noise conditions
used in evaluation.  The resulting behavior is best described as
\emph{fixed inference across an in-distribution fading ensemble}.  No
per-realization retraining occurs, but this does not demonstrate transfer to a
structurally unseen channel family.

Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels} extends the distinction.  Its Layer~4
network is trained over a family and evaluated on fresh per-block realizations
from that family.  This is within-family amortized inference.  Pilot-aided MLSE
instead estimates each block, and CMA adapts online within each block.  These
three mechanisms should not all be called adaptation.

\section{Capacity and Overfitting}
\label{sec:capacity-and-overfitting}

\subsection{What H3 does and does not establish}

The stronger H3 claim, that excess capacity produces an upward overfitting arm,
is not supported.  The dedicated sweep evaluated 152 configurations with
three initializations per configuration.  The median within-configuration
spread was \(0.180\)~dB; \(57\%\) of configurations exceeded \(0.15\)~dB; and
the worst spread was \(3.18\)~dB for an eleven-parameter relay on complex ISI.
The canonical channel was highly reproducible, with a median spread of
\(0.008\)~dB.  The nonlinear-bias channel exhibited approximately
\(1.23\)~dB two-outcome behavior rather than a smooth capacity-dependent trend.

Against that initialization scale, no robust upward arm was observed.
Parameter count was largely idle on the evaluated memoryless channels.
The composite study leaves room for a small benefit from additional capacity
near 10~dB, but it does not overturn the main result and does not establish a
general monotone size benefit either.  Accordingly, the defensible conclusion
is saturation or idle capacity, not an optimal bias--variance point at exactly
169 parameters.

Minimum-description-length and bias--variance language may be retained only
as heuristic interpretation.  The memoryless target is low-dimensional and
therefore plausibly needs little representational capacity, but the thesis did
not measure bias and variance separately, nor did it demonstrate memorization
through a train--test gap.

\subsection{The resource that becomes binding}
\label{sec:complexity-curve-turn}

The sweep identifies temporal context, rather than raw parameter count, as the
binding architectural resource on the evaluated channels with memory.
Memoryless channels need little context.  On the real three-tap ISI channel,
the SNR penalty falls from \(+12.95\) to \(+3.60\), \(+2.75\), and
\(+1.67\)~dB as the window grows from \(1\) to \(3\), \(5\), and \(7\).
This measured progression is retained without extrapolating it to window~11.

The result is deliberately narrow.  It does not imply that channel memory is
the only source of architectural complexity: nonlinearities, constellation
and output parameterization, coding interfaces, and optimization can also
matter.  It establishes that temporal context is the binding resource in the
evaluated memory channels, while width and depth are secondary over the tested
range.

No additional structural claim is made that a one-sample network can never
represent the unknown-phase task.  Differential detection normally depends on
a relation between consecutive received samples, but an impossibility claim
would require the exact training inputs, state convention, and detector target
to be formalized.  The empirical window result is sufficient.

\subsection{Architecture and initialization evidence}

The original broad canonical comparison remains incompletely controlled:
architectures had unequal sizes or training budgets and were initially
represented by individual runs.  Later follow-ups provide stronger evidence
for narrower comparisons.  Equal-budget architectures were retrained on the
memory channels under common hyperparameters and three initializations; the
feedforward MLP was strongest on every tested memory/composite channel, while
per-architecture tuning was not performed.

The canonical Transformer follow-up first showed a three-seed spread of
\(0.906\)~dB.  In the eight-run investigation, seven runs converged and one did
not.  The failed run ended at loss \(0.0256\), compared with
\(0.0018\)--\(0.0023\) for the converged runs.  Across all eight, loss and SNR
penalty correlated at \(r=+0.998\), but this was driven by the outlier; within
the converged runs, \(r=-0.38\) with \(p=0.41\).  Validation accuracy did not
separate the failed run from the healthy ones.  Final loss therefore detected
the observed non-convergence and could serve as a convergence diagnostic in
this experiment.  Eight trials do not support a population failure-rate
estimate, and removing all future instability by seed selection is not
established.

\subsection{Practical implications}

The practical lesson is to size the learned relay around the temporal and
output structure required by the channel, rather than around a generic
``larger is safer'' rule.  For reference, 169 float32 parameters occupy
\(169\times4=676\) bytes for weights alone.  This arithmetic excludes model
metadata, buffers, code, alignment, and implementation overhead, and therefore
does not by itself prove a particular SRAM-only deployment or absence of
external memory access.  Likewise, training-time measurements characterize
the reported reference implementation and hardware, not universal deployment
latency.

\subsection{Latency and adaptivity}
\label{sec:latency-and-adaptivity-considerations}

Latency has several distinct components.  The half-duplex protocol may already
buffer a listening-phase block.  A symmetric learned window of length \(W\)
has structural look-ahead \(\lfloor W/2\rfloor\); therefore \(W=11\) requires
five future symbols, not ten.  Compute latency depends on the implementation.
MLSE adds traceback delay, while block-DF must buffer and decode an entire
frame before forwarding.

On the measured coded \(L=3\) channel, MLSE is more accurate, cheaper in
MACs, and no slower than the \(W=11\) learned relay.  At 12~dB, MLSE used
128 MACs per symbol and achieved BER \(3.3\times10^{-5}\); the learned relay
used 208 MACs per symbol, five symbols of look-ahead, and achieved
\(1.89\times10^{-4}\).  Equal measured BER at traceback depths 2, 3, and 15
does not by itself prove a particular survivor-merging event.

For the evaluated QPSK architecture,
\[
    C_{\mathrm{MLSE}}=2M^L,\qquad
    C_{\mathrm{MLP}}=2WH+4H.
\]
With \(M=4\), \(W=11\), and \(H=8\), the arithmetic crossover lies between
\(L=3\) and \(L=4\): 128 versus 208 MACs at \(L=3\), and 512 versus 208 at
\(L=4\).  This is architecture-specific.  The learned cost is not independent
of memory in general because a longer channel may require a wider window or
different model; the supported claim is the gentler growth observed for the
evaluated windowed MLP.  Block-DF is separate again: the measured frame
contains 202 symbols, so its buffering dominates even before its decoding
arithmetic is considered.

Finally, multi-SNR training, within-family inference, and online adaptation
must remain distinct.  The learned relay is fixed at inference.  CMA adapts
online.  Pilot-aided MLSE estimates each block and then performs model-based
sequence detection.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The canonical conclusions are limited to the matched, uncoded
    QPSK/Rayleigh setting and its evaluated SNR range.

    \item The unknown-channel study covers finite impairment families and
    memory lengths.  Layer~4 redraws realizations per block, but structurally
    unseen test families, broader nonlinearities, and longer or time-varying
    memory remain untested.

    \item The systematic window sweep removes ``fixed window size'' as a
    limitation, but only windows and channel memories in the reported finite
    range were evaluated.

    \item The coding study includes block-DF, soft information, MCS selection,
    latency, and joint latency--memory measurements, but uses one code family,
    finite frames, and limited channel models.  It includes no iterative
    relay--destination exchange and demonstrates no URLLC-level reliability.

    \item The principal learned relays are trained offline.  Within-family
    amortization does not establish tracking under distribution drift.

    \item The original broad cross-architecture comparison remains
    single-run or unequal-budget in important respects.  Seed evidence now
    covers the MLP size sweep, equal-budget memory-channel comparison, and the
    focused Transformer follow-up, but not every original architecture and
    training-protocol combination.

    \item The study remains single-relay, two-hop, half-duplex, and SISO.
    Multi-relay coordination, additional hops, direct links, full-duplex
    operation, and MIMO are outside its evidence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Time-selective channels and broader estimation mismatch.}
    Study Doppler, phase drift, estimator bias, nonstationary block statistics,
    train--test family mismatch, and joint estimator--relay adaptation.

    \item \textbf{Higher-order constellations.}
    Evaluate dense QAM/PSK using joint classification, regression,
    probabilistic soft-symbol outputs, or hierarchical heads.  The current
    per-axis formulation does not establish performance for dense
    constellations, but such constellations do not necessarily require a single
    \(M\)-class formulation.

    \item \textbf{BER-optimal classical margin.}
    Add BCJR/APP detection on the memory channel to measure the bit-error-optimal
    classical margin.  This is an optional refinement of the benchmark, not a
    missing explanation for the corrected canonical QPSK result.

    \item \textbf{Coded extensions.}
    Evaluate other codes, frame lengths, and channels; calibrate soft block-DF
    with per-symbol CSI; examine iterative relay--destination exchange; and
    measure the reliability--latency trade-off at substantially lower target
    error rates.

    \item \textbf{Longer-context architectures.}
    Test tuned feedforward, convolutional, recurrent, Transformer, and
    state-space relays at longer context.  Because the feedforward MLP
    outperformed Transformer and both Mamba variants near 3K parameters under
    the common protocol on the tested memory channels, improved state-space
    performance is an open architecture-specific question, not an expected
    outcome.

    \item \textbf{Implementation-aware evaluation.}
    Compare optimized classical and learned implementations on matched hardware,
    including memory traffic, quantization, energy, throughput, and causal
    versus symmetric-window operation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Hypotheses Revisited}

\begin{description}
    \item[H1: Confirmed in scope.] Minimal learned relays outperform AF at
    low SNR on the canonical channel; no general learned advantage over DF is
    established.

    \item[H2: Confirmed in scope.] In the matched memoryless canonical setting,
    symbol-wise DF matches or exceeds the learned relays from medium to high
    SNR, with only a small measured SNR-penalty margin.

    \item[H3: Strong form not supported.] No robust upward overfitting arm was
    found.  The weaker saturation/idle-capacity result is supported, while
    temporal context becomes binding on the evaluated channels with memory.

    \item[H4: Qualified.] The original broad canonical architecture comparison
    is not fully seed-robust or budget-matched.  Under the evaluated equal-budget
    memory-channel protocol, the feedforward MLP remains strongest; the
    Transformer follow-up further shows that convergence diagnostics matter.

    \item[H5: Supported in scoped form.] Windowed learned processing restores
    links that memoryless relays lose and occupies a niche when per-block
    identification is unreliable, unavailable, or too costly.  Correctly
    informed MLSE remains more accurate where it applies and can be afforded;
    long-block CMA closely approaches the learned relay without pilots.
\end{description}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The thesis supports a boundary, not a universal ranking.  Classical methods
dominate when their model is identifiable and their arithmetic is affordable.
Learned relaying becomes useful when per-block identification is unreliable or
unavailable, or when exact model-based sequence processing exceeds the
available resource budget.  On the evaluated memory channels, temporal context
rather than raw parameter count is the key learned resource.  The learned
relay's cost grows with that context and with its output parameterization, even
though it grows more gently than an exact trellis in the studied comparison.

Accordingly, memory does not make classical theory obsolete: it invalidates
memoryless relay functions while leaving correctly informed sequence detection
strong.  Finite pilot information introduces an estimation-quality crossover;
blind long blocks admit competitive CMA processing; short blind blocks create
a narrower niche for amortized fixed learned inference; and higher-memory or
higher-order cases introduce a separate arithmetic boundary.  None of these
results establishes universal learned superiority.  Together they delineate
when learned relaying complements classical signal processing and when it
should defer to it.
