\chapter{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

\paragraph{Scope of the summarized evidence.}
This thesis contains three distinct evidence blocks.
First, the canonical study in Chapter~\ref{sec:experiments} is a
matched-model, memoryless, uncoded QPSK/Rayleigh control in which the relay
function is the principal varied component.  It primarily settles H1--H4.
Second, the supplementary study in
Section~\ref{sec:coded-block-df} introduces convolutional coding,
block decode-and-forward (block-DF), soft-information forwarding,
modulation-and-coding adaptation, and explicit resource and latency
constraints.  It is a system-level extension, not an additional layer of
channel information.  Third, Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels} contains
Layers~2--4 of the unknown/mismatched-channel study and provides the
principal experimental evidence for H5.  In that chapter, Viterbi MLSE
denotes sequence detection for a channel with memory; it is distinct from
Viterbi decoding of the convolutional code in the supplementary coded
study.  The symbol-wise and block meanings of DF remain as defined in
Remark~\ref{rem:df-terminology}.

\paragraph{Thesis objective and organizing result.}
The objective was to determine where learned relay processing usefully
complements classical processing in a two-hop link, rather than to establish
a universal learned-over-classical ranking.  The answer is a two-axis regime
map.  The \emph{identifiability} axis asks whether an accurate channel model
or reliable per-block estimate is available.  The
\emph{arithmetic/resource-feasibility} axis asks whether the corresponding
exact model-based processor is affordable once channel memory, modulation
order, buffering, and delay are included.  These axes are not mutually
exclusive research objectives: they expose different boundaries that
jointly determine the appropriate relay strategy, as posed in
Section~\ref{sec:research-objectives} and interpreted in
Chapter~\ref{sec:discussion-and-conclusions}.

\paragraph{Principal findings.}
The final findings are as follows.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Low-SNR advantage over AF is confirmed in scope (H1).}
    On the canonical matched QPSK/Rayleigh channel, the best evaluated
    learned relays outperform amplify-and-forward at low SNR.  This is a
    comparison with AF, not evidence that learned per-symbol relays generally
    outperform DF.

    \item \textbf{Matched symbol-wise DF remains the stronger medium/high-SNR
    reference (H2).}
    In the canonical memoryless setting, symbol-wise DF matches or exceeds
    the learned per-symbol relays from medium to high SNR while requiring no
    learned parameters or training.  This empirical conclusion is confined
    to the matched, uncoded, per-symbol experiment and is not a general
    information-theoretic optimality claim.

    \item \textbf{The strong overfitting form of H3 is not supported.}
    The dedicated sweep evaluated 152 configurations with three
    initializations per configuration.  The median within-configuration
    initialization spread was \(0.180\,\mathrm{dB}\), and no robust upward
    BER-versus-capacity arm was resolved above that variability.  The
    supported conclusion is therefore saturation or idle capacity over the
    evaluated range, not a demonstrated bias--variance optimum or harmful
    excess capacity.

    \item \textbf{Temporal context, rather than raw parameter count, is the
    binding learned resource on the evaluated memory channels.}
    On the canonical memoryless channel, a four-parameter relay lies near
    the canonical baseline.  On the evaluated three-tap channels, meeting
    the strict reported degradation criterion requires \(73\)--\(145\)
    parameters together with window coverage of the interference.  The
    important distinction is the window, not the parameter count alone:
    widening the window substantially reduces the penalty on channels with
    memory, whereas additional width or depth is largely idle once the
    required context is present.  This is the result of
    Sections~\ref{sec:minimum-relay-size} and
    \ref{sec:complexity-curve-turn}; it is not a claim that learned cost is
    independent of channel memory.

    \item \textbf{The compact canonical relay is sufficient, but not uniquely
    optimal.}
    The canonical \(169\)-parameter MLP attains essentially the same BER as
    much larger evaluated models.  The size sweep strengthens the conclusion
    that extra capacity is often unused, but it does not identify
    \(169\) parameters as a universal optimum, nor does it show that smaller
    models are intrinsically more robust.

    \item \textbf{Architecture conclusions are qualified (H4).}
    The original broad canonical comparison is not fully seed-robust or
    budget-matched, and equalizing parameter count does not equalize all
    architectural and optimization choices.  Under the later common-protocol,
    equal-budget study on the evaluated memory channels, the feedforward MLP
    is strongest.  The focused Transformer follow-up shows that convergence
    diagnostics matter: a non-converged initialization must not be treated as
    evidence for a general architecture ranking.  Architectural differences
    therefore cannot be said to have ``largely vanished'' in general.

    \item \textbf{The matched control defines the baseline from which H5 must
    be read.}
    Parameter uncertainty alone does not establish a learned advantage.
    Memory breaks memoryless relay functions, but it does not invalidate
    correctly informed sequence detection.  The principal contribution of
    Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels} is consequently a delineation of the
    conditions under which the preconditions of model-based processing cease
    to hold, rather than a claim that learning becomes universally necessary.

    \item \textbf{Layers~2 and 4 separate fixed channel memory, composite
    mismatch, and blind within-family inference (H5).}
    In the fixed three-tap BPSK ISI experiment of Layer~2, the analytic
    \(0.25\) floor applies only to the stated tap vector
    \(\mathbf{h}\propto[1,\,0.7,\,0.5]\) and the evaluated memoryless-relay
    class.  A \(170\)-parameter windowed MLP restores a decreasing BER curve
    and reaches \(4.79\times10^{-8}\) at \(16\,\mathrm{dB}\), approximately
    \(1\)--\(1.5\,\mathrm{dB}\) behind exact-CSI MLSE.  Thus MLSE remains the
    stronger detector when the channel is known and the trellis is
    affordable.

    The fixed composite experiment separately shows that a compact learned
    relay can process the evaluated ISI, amplifier-nonlinearity, and phase
    combination without an explicit analytic impairment model at inference;
    this does not mean that the impairment structure was absent from
    training.  In Layer~4, the channel realization is redrawn per block from
    the same parametric family used in training.  On sufficiently long blind
    blocks, the learned relay and corrected CMA track closely rather than
    match exactly: at \(20\,\mathrm{dB}\), their BERs are \(0.00262\) and
    \(0.00329\), respectively.  CMA adapts online within the block, whereas
    the MLP performs fixed, amortized within-family inference.  Structurally
    unseen channel families were not tested.

    \item \textbf{Finite channel information creates an estimation-quality
    crossover (Layer~3).}
    ``Finite channel information'' denotes the pilots and resulting channel
    estimate supplied to the classical detector; no Bayesian posterior is
    claimed to have been computed.  At \(10\,\mathrm{dB}\), pilot-aided MLSE
    retains its BER advantage with \(20\) pilots but has lost it by
    \(10\) pilots, locating the measured crossover between those two budgets.
    The learned relay uses no per-block pilots, but it is trained on the same
    parametric channel family and evaluated on unseen realizations from that
    family.  It is therefore not model-agnostic to an unknown structural
    family.

    \item \textbf{Arithmetic supplies the second boundary.}
    Exact MLSE for constellation order \(M\) and \(L\) channel taps has
    \(M^{L-1}\) states and evaluates \(M^L\) branches per symbol.  Under the
    MAC convention used in Section~\ref{sec:joint-latency-memory},
    \[
        C_{\mathrm{MLSE}} = 2M^L,
        \qquad
        C_{\mathrm{MLP}} = 2WH + 4H
    \]
    for the evaluated QPSK windowed MLP.  With \(M=4\), \(W=11\), and
    \(H=8\), the continuous crossover is
    \[
        L^\star
        = \log_M\!\left(\frac{2WH+4H}{2}\right)
        = 3.35,
    \]
    so the practical integer crossover lies between \(L=3\) and \(L=4\).
    At \(L=3\), MLSE costs \(128\) MACs per symbol against \(208\) for the
    MLP and also has the measured accuracy and delay advantage.  At \(L=7\),
    the corresponding costs are \(32{,}768\) and \(208\) MACs, a ratio of
    approximately \(157.5\times\).  This ratio is architecture- and
    operating-point-specific.  Learned arithmetic is not generally
    memory-independent: a longer channel may require a wider window, more
    hidden units, or a different output parameterization.

    \item \textbf{Latency does not favor the learned detector on the measured
    three-tap coded channel.}
    On that configuration, MLSE is more accurate, cheaper in MACs, and no
    slower than the \(W=11\) MLP.  No BER improvement was resolved between
    the tested traceback depths \(D=2\), \(3\), and \(15\); this observation
    does not establish a causal survivor-merging depth.  The symmetric
    \(W=11\) MLP requires five symbols of look-ahead.  Block-DF requires
    \(202\) symbols of frame buffering, while block-DF followed by MLSE has
    \(205\) symbols of measured structural delay.  On this measured
    channel/configuration, the added block buffering does not improve on the
    MLSE BER; no universal statement about block-DF latency or accuracy
    follows.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Supplementary coded-study synthesis.}
The coded/resource study in Section~\ref{sec:coded-block-df} measures rather
than assumes the effects of block-DF, soft-information forwarding, adaptive
modulation and coding, and buffering latency.  On its measured code, frame,
channel, and SNR grid, block-DF overtakes uncoded symbol-wise DF from
\(8\,\mathrm{dB}\) upward; Mamba-S6 overtakes block-DF at \(16\,\mathrm{dB}\),
and both evaluated learned relays do so by \(20\,\mathrm{dB}\).  These are
grid-specific crossovers, not universal coded-relay rankings.  Once the MCS
is selected jointly, rate selection has a larger effect on delivered
goodput than the measured difference between relay-decoding strategies,
while a latency constraint can penalize block buffering.  The study covers
one finite code/frame/channel family and therefore motivates, rather than
settles, broader coded-relay deployment questions.

\paragraph{Overall conclusion.}
The thesis supports learning-based relays as targeted complements, not
universal replacements.  Classical methods dominate when the applicable
model is identifiable and the corresponding processor is affordable.
Learned relaying becomes useful when reliable per-block identification is
unavailable or unreliable, or when exact model-based processing exceeds the
available arithmetic or buffering budget.  On the evaluated channels with
memory, temporal context rather than raw parameter count is the key learned
resource, and its cost must be counted rather than treated as constant.
Long blind blocks admit competitive corrected CMA processing; the narrower
learned niche is fixed, amortized inference over the trained family when
per-block identification or adaptation is impractical.  Higher-order
constellations remain future work and may be addressed through joint
classification, regression, probabilistic soft-symbol outputs, hierarchical
heads, or other formulations.  Structurally unseen channel families remain
outside the evidence.
