\chapter{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:literature-review}

This chapter reviews the bodies of work needed to ask when a learned relay
can usefully complement classical relay processing. The organizing question is
not whether neural processing is preferable in general. It is whether a
model-based rule can be instantiated from the information available at the
relay, and whether that rule can be executed within the relevant computation
and decision-delay budgets.

Unless stated otherwise, the reference configuration is the canonical
benchmark defined in Chapter~\ref{sec:introduction}: a half-duplex,
source--relay--destination link with one relay, no direct
source--destination path, SISO transmission on both hops, independent
Rayleigh fast fading, complex baseband signaling, Gray-coded QPSK, and
uncoded BER as the performance metric. The receiver-side channel information
required by the matched coherent detector is available in this benchmark, and
the relay-processing function is the varied block. Channel memory, uncertain
channel estimates, blind processing, and coding are reviewed because they
relax particular assumptions of that control, not because they redefine it.

\section{Classical Relay Foundations}
\label{sec:classical-relay-strategies}

Relay-channel theory includes amplify-and-forward (AF), decode-and-forward
(DF), compress-and-forward, estimate-and-forward, and related cooperative
strategies \cite{Laneman2004CooperativeDiversity,
TseViswanath2005Fundamentals,Nosratinia2004CooperativeCommunication}.
Their guarantees depend on the topology, channel law, coding assumptions,
available channel state information (CSI), and optimization criterion.
Consequently, a statement that a relay is ``optimal'' is meaningful only
after those conditions have been specified.

\subsection{Amplify-and-Forward}
\label{sec:classical-relay-strategies-af}

AF applies a linear gain to the relay observation,
\begin{equation}
  x_R[i] = G[i]y_R[i],
  \qquad
  G[i] =
  \sqrt{\frac{P_R}{\mathbb{E}\!\left[|y_R[i]|^2
  \mid \mathcal{I}_R[i]\right]}},
  \label{eq:af-gain}
\end{equation}
where $P_R$ is the relay transmit-power constraint and
$\mathcal{I}_R[i]$ denotes the information used to set the gain. For the
CSI-assisted variable-gain convention and independent hops, the familiar
instantaneous end-to-end SNR is
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_{\mathrm{AF}}
  =
  \frac{\gamma_1\gamma_2}
       {\gamma_1+\gamma_2+1}.
  \label{eq:af-snr}
\end{equation}
This expression is not interchangeable with a fixed-gain result. Its scope
includes the gain convention and the CSI needed to apply it. AF avoids a
decoding operation, but forwards first-hop noise and channel distortion
together with the desired signal.

\subsection{Decode-and-Forward}
\label{sec:classical-relay-strategies-df}

The term DF has two materially different uses. In relay information theory,
DF normally denotes a block strategy: the source channel-encodes a message,
the relay decodes a codeword or message block, and the relay subsequently
re-encodes it for the second hop. Achievable-rate and capacity results for
such schemes concern specified relay-channel models and asymptotic or
sufficiently long coded blocks. They do not directly predict uncoded BER at
the finite operating points studied in the canonical benchmark.

The canonical baseline in this thesis is instead \emph{uncoded symbol-wise
DF}. After coherent compensation, the relay makes an independent Gray-QPSK
decision and remodulates it:
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\boldsymbol b}_R[i]
  =
  \operatorname{demod}\!\left(\widetilde y_R[i]\right),
  \qquad
  x_R[i]
  =
  \operatorname{mod}\!\left(\widehat{\boldsymbol b}_R[i]\right).
  \label{eq:df-demod-remod}
\end{equation}
For independent binary error events on the two hops, its bit-error
composition is
\begin{equation}
  P_e^{\mathrm{DF}}
  =
  P_{e,1}(1-P_{e,2})
  +(1-P_{e,1})P_{e,2}.
  \label{eq:df-ber-hops}
\end{equation}
This is a property of the uncoded symbol-wise model. It is not a bound on
coded block DF, soft-information relaying, joint sequence processing, or
other relay functions.

Under a known memoryless channel, equiprobable QPSK, and the per-symbol
probability-of-error criterion, componentwise nearest-neighbor slicing after
coherent compensation is the MAP hard decision. This scoped statement
explains why symbol-wise DF is a strong matched-model control. It does not
establish that hard slicing is the globally optimal end-to-end relay mapping
under every power, coding, latency, or information constraint.

\subsection{Other Classical Strategies}

Compress-and-forward and estimate-and-forward retain or describe uncertainty
rather than committing to decoded symbols, while selective and hybrid
protocols vary relay behavior with channel quality
\cite{Laneman2004CooperativeDiversity,TseViswanath2005Fundamentals}.
These strategies show that AF and DF are representative endpoints, not
universal lower and upper bounds. The present thesis uses them in the
canonical comparison because they provide transparent linear-forwarding and
hard-regeneration references while leaving the source, channels, and
destination fixed.

\section{Canonical Channel and Detection Assumptions}
\label{sec:theoretical-foundations-channel-models}

For one canonical hop,
\begin{equation}
  y[i]=h[i]x[i]+n[i],
  \qquad
  h[i]\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1),
  \qquad
  n[i]\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,N_0),
  \label{eq:rayleigh-channel}
\end{equation}
with independent fading and noise across hops and channel uses. Each
receiver knows the coefficient required for coherent compensation. With
unit-average-power Gray-coded QPSK, the compensated in-phase and quadrature
components form two binary nearest-neighbor decisions. The stated SNR
convention must therefore be kept consistent with whether it denotes
$E_s/N_0$ or $E_b/N_0$ \cite{ProakisSalehi2008DigitalComm}.

For coherent binary detection over Rayleigh fading, averaging the conditional
error probability over the exponential instantaneous SNR yields
\begin{equation}
  P_e^{\mathrm{Rayleigh}}
  =
  \frac{1}{2}
  \left(
    1-\sqrt{\frac{\bar{\gamma}}{1+\bar{\gamma}}}
  \right),
  \label{eq:rayleigh-ber}
\end{equation}
under the corresponding textbook convention
\cite{ProakisSalehi2008DigitalComm}. In this thesis such expressions serve as
matched-model calibration references. They do not imply that fading itself
creates an opportunity for learning. When $h[i]$ is supplied and the channel
is memoryless, the sufficient observation and the classical decision rule
are already known.

This point separates two questions that are otherwise easy to conflate.
A channel may be statistically random yet identifiable at each decision
because its needed realization is supplied. Conversely, even a simple
finite-dimensional channel can be difficult to use with a model-based rule
when its coefficients, memory, or impairment family are unavailable or
poorly estimated.

\section{Neural Methods for the Physical Layer}
\label{sec:machine-learning-in-wireless-communication}

Neural physical-layer research spans end-to-end transceiver optimization,
channel estimation, equalization, detection, decoding, and resource
allocation. O'Shea and Hoydis formulated a communication link as an
autoencoder and also emphasized architectures that incorporate domain
knowledge \cite{OSheaHoydis2017PhysicalLayer}. Ye et al.\ demonstrated a
data-driven OFDM receiver that combines implicit channel estimation and
symbol recovery, including tests with sparse pilots and model impairments
\cite{YeLiJuang2018OFDMDL}. Farsad and Goldsmith studied neural sequence
detection when an accurate channel model or instantaneous CSI is unavailable
\cite{FarsadGoldsmith2018SequenceDetection}.

Recent surveys organize this literature by physical-layer task and neural
architecture, while also highlighting data dependence, generalization,
interpretability, computation, and compatibility with model-based receivers
\cite{YeEtAl2024AI4PHY,DohaAbdelhadi2025ReceiverSurvey}. Yu, Sohrabi, and
Jiang argue that the useful role of deep learning depends on matching the
architecture to the structure of the communication problem
\cite{YuSohrabiJiang2022RoleDL}. These works motivate learned processing
where mappings are difficult to specify or execute. They do not imply an
advantage over a correctly specified, inexpensive detector.

\subsection{Learned Relay Functions}

A supervised relay can learn a local mapping from a received observation or
window to a clean-symbol estimate,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{x}[i]
  =
  f_{\theta}\!\left(\boldsymbol y_{i-w:i+w}\right),
  \qquad
  \mathcal{L}(\theta)
  =
  \frac{1}{N}
  \sum_{i=1}^{N}
  \left|
    \widehat{x}[i]-x[i]
  \right|^2.
  \label{eq:mse-loss}
\end{equation}
For squared loss, the population minimizer is the posterior mean associated
with the training distribution. This is an MSE statement, not a proof of
end-to-end BER optimality. On the canonical i.i.d.\ memoryless channel,
neighboring samples supply no additional information about $x[i]$ once the
current compensated sample is known. A window becomes functionally relevant
only when the true channel or code couples symbols across time.

The architectures evaluated later represent several inductive biases. An MLP
implements a finite-window nonlinear map. A variational autoencoder and a
conditional generative adversarial network introduce latent-variable or
distribution-matching objectives \cite{KingmaWelling2014VAE,
MirzaOsindero2014cGAN,Goodfellow2014GAN,Gulrajani2017WGANgp}. Their inclusion
tests whether a generative objective is useful for relay reconstruction; the
literature alone gives no reason to assume that it is.

Transformers use content-dependent pairwise interactions,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V)
  =
  \operatorname{softmax}
  \left(
    \frac{QK^{\mathsf T}}{\sqrt{d_k}}
  \right)V,
  \label{eq:attention}
\end{equation}
whereas structured state-space models maintain a learned state,
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol s_k
  =
  \overline A_k\boldsymbol s_{k-1}
  +\overline B_k u_k,
  \qquad
  z_k=C_k\boldsymbol s_k+D u_k.
  \label{eq:ssm-discrete}
\end{equation}
Transformers, S4, Mamba, and Mamba-2 provide different mechanisms for using
context and different scaling with sequence length
\cite{Vaswani2017Attention,GuGoelRe2022SSM,gu2023mamba,
DaoGu2024TransformersSSM}. These mechanisms may matter on channels with
memory. On a memoryless control, sequence capacity is an architectural
degree of freedom rather than additional channel information.

Related relay-learning work often addresses relay selection, power or
resource allocation, or analog gain optimization rather than learning the
symbol-level relay mapping itself
\cite{Gunduz2019MLAir,Akdemir2024DLRelaySelection,
Bergel2023RelaysAsNeurons,BergelICASSP2024}. This distinction is important:
selecting a conventional relay and replacing its local detector are different
problems with different baselines.

\section{Channel Memory and Sequence Detection}
\label{sec:sequence-detection-background}

A finite-memory channel can be written as
\begin{equation}
  y[i]
  =
  \sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}g_{\ell}x[i-\ell]+n[i].
  \label{eq:finite-memory-channel}
\end{equation}
The observation at one time now depends on multiple transmitted symbols, so a
memoryless slicer is generally not the matched detector. If the channel
coefficients, alphabet, memory, and noise model are known, maximum-likelihood
sequence estimation searches the finite-state trellis for the most likely
transmitted sequence. The Viterbi algorithm performs this search by dynamic
programming \cite{Forney1972MLSE,Forney1973Viterbi}. Its optimality is
therefore ML sequence optimality for the specified finite-memory model and
branch metric, not universal bit-error optimality.

When the desired criterion is marginal symbol or bit error, a
forward--backward MAP computation is the relevant distinction. The BCJR
algorithm computes state and transition posteriors and supports
symbol-by-symbol MAP decisions or soft outputs
\cite{BahlCockeJelinekRaviv1974}. Viterbi and BCJR consequently answer
different estimation questions even when they operate on the same trellis.

The trellis state count grows with the constellation and channel memory.
This gives a known-model detector a computational precondition in addition
to its informational precondition. Learned sequence detectors illustrate a
third design point. Farsad and Goldsmith use a sliding bidirectional recurrent
network for sequence detection without relying on an explicit analytical
channel model \cite{FarsadGoldsmith2018SequenceDetection}, while ViterbiNet
retains the Viterbi recursion and learns only the model-dependent quantities
\cite{Shlezinger2020ViterbiNet}. These examples motivate comparisons among
model-based, model-free, and hybrid function classes rather than a binary
classical-versus-neural comparison.

\section{Channel Estimation, Mismatch, and Blind Processing}
\label{sec:channel-estimation-background}

A classical detector may require a channel family and instantaneous
parameters. Pilots can estimate those parameters, but their quality depends
on pilot count, excitation, SNR, estimator assumptions, and channel
variation. A receiver based on $\widehat{\boldsymbol g}$ is thus matched to
the estimated model, not necessarily to the physical channel. Reduced pilot
budgets affect both estimation variance and payload overhead. These effects
should not be described simply as ``unknown channel'' without stating what
is known, estimated, or assumed.

Blind equalizers infer a compensating filter from received data without a
training sequence. The constant modulus algorithm (CMA), for example, uses a
constant-envelope criterion and is especially natural for constant-modulus
signaling \cite{Godard1980SelfRecoveringEqualization}. Its behavior depends
on channel conditions, adaptation choices, initialization, and data length.
Accordingly, CMA and decision-directed equalization are treated in this
thesis as evaluated blind baselines, not as universally strongest blind
methods.

Neural receivers can amortize inference over a training distribution: the
cost of learning a mapping is paid offline and the deployed model applies
that mapping without an explicit per-block identification stage. This can be
useful when the operational distribution is represented in training, but it
does not remove distribution assumptions. Robustness to realizations from a
trained family should not be confused with robustness to unobserved channel
families.

\section{Coding and Resource Considerations}
\label{sec:coding-background}

Channel coding changes both the relay task and the comparison criterion. A
rate-$R$ code introduces redundancy across a block, and a genuine block-DF
relay decodes that block before re-encoding it for the second hop. A
denoise-only relay instead modifies noisy coded symbols without necessarily
decoding the message. Soft outputs can preserve reliability information that
hard regenerated symbols discard. Modern neural-coding work likewise spans
learned decoders, model-driven unfolding, and learned code design
\cite{MatsumineOchiai2024CodingSurvey}.

A coded comparison must account for more than BER. With constellation size
$M$, the nominal spectral efficiency is
\begin{equation}
  \eta=R\log_2 M
  \quad \text{information bits per channel symbol}.
  \label{eq:spectral-efficiency}
\end{equation}
Puncturing, termination, pilot symbols, retransmissions, and half-duplex use
can further change effective throughput. Block decoding also introduces
buffering and decision delay, while Viterbi, BCJR, and neural inference have
different arithmetic and memory costs. Capacity results provide important
asymptotic reference points, but do not by themselves rank finite-length
schemes at a fixed uncoded-BER operating point.

For this reason, the coded/resource study in Chapter~5 is supplementary. It
tests whether the symbol-wise DF reference remains informative once coding,
soft information, throughput normalization, and latency are admitted. It is
not an additional layer of channel information in the identifiability
argument of Chapter~\ref{sec:unknown-channels}.

\section{Synthesis and Research Gap}
\label{sec:literature-synthesis-gap}

The literature establishes two complementary facts. First, classical
receivers are strong when the channel law, required parameters, and objective
are specified: coherent slicing solves the matched memoryless symbol
decision, Viterbi performs ML sequence estimation for a known finite-memory
model, and BCJR supplies marginal MAP information on a known trellis.
Second, learned and hybrid receivers can represent difficult mappings, absorb
model-dependent computations into training, and operate without an explicit
instantaneous model in settings demonstrated by their training and
evaluation distributions.

What prior work does not determine for the relay problem is where one fact
ceases to dominate the other when assumptions are relaxed systematically.
Four issues must be separated: matched-model performance, availability and
estimability of the information needed by the classical rule, the function
class required by channel memory or distortion, and the arithmetic,
buffering, and decision delay needed to execute the rule.

This separation gives the thesis its two boundaries. The
\emph{identifiability boundary} asks whether the information needed to
instantiate the model-based detector is supplied or can be estimated
reliably. The \emph{arithmetic boundary} asks whether that detector can be
executed within computation and delay budgets. The canonical
QPSK/Rayleigh experiment is the matched-model control; Layers~2--4 then vary
channel memory and information availability, while the coded study separately
examines resource and block-processing consequences.

The resulting research gap is therefore not a missing demonstration that
neural relays can process noisy signals. It is the absence of a controlled
account of where learning becomes useful once model information, required
function class, and resource assumptions are varied without treating them as
one effect. The next chapter turns this gap into the thesis objectives and
the comparisons needed to locate those boundaries.
